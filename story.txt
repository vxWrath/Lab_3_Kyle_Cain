The radio chatter in Marcus’s earpiece buzzed with static as he leaned over the ledge of the sky-rise. Twelve stories below, a fractured water main had turned the downtown intersection into a churning, violent whirlpool. A stranded school bus tilted dangerously on the buckling asphalt, its rear tires slipping into the rushing current.

Marcus closed his eyes and breathed in, reaching for the spark beneath his ribs.

He didn't wear armor or fly through the clouds. His power was raw, kinetic resonance—the ability to manipulate vibration and momentum. To the city, he was **Vanguard**, a blur of copper light in the dark. To himself, he was just a man trying to balance a heavy debt to the world.

He stepped off the ledge.

Wind roared in his ears as gravity pulled him down. Right before impact, Marcus released a localized pulse beneath his boots. The kinetic force absorbed his momentum, letting him land silently on the wet pavement without breaking a bone.

The bus gave a sickening screech. The street beneath it was dissolving fast. Inside, terrified faces pressed against the glass.

"Hold on!" Marcus shouted, his voice amplified by the resonance humming through his throat.

He lunged beneath the front bumper, pressing his palms flat against the ice-cold steel. The weight was crushing, threatening to flatten his spine. He channeled the energy of the rushing river, absorbing the impact of the crashing water and converting it into pure, upward lift.

Veins of glowing orange light surged across his arms and shoulders. With a deep, guttural roar, Marcus forced the kinetic energy outward, arresting the vehicle’s slide. He locked his legs, driving his boots into the solid concrete behind him, and pulled the thirty-ton bus backward out of the sinkhole inch by agonizing inch.

The metal groaned, then settled onto stable pavement. Marcus collapsed to his knees, gasping for air as his glowing skin faded back to normal.

A quiet cheer broke out from inside the bus, followed by the distant wail of approaching sirens. Marcus looked up, caught the eye of the crying bus driver, and gave a weary, reassuring nod.

The city had a thousand problems, but tonight, thirty kids were going home. He pushed himself up, vanished into the shadows of the alleyway, and waited for the next call.
